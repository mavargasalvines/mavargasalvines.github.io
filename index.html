About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at Virginia Tech. My research lies at the intersection of labor economics, the economics of education, and applied microeconometrics, with a focus on the school-to-work transition and on how policy shapes labor markets and educational outcomes in the United States and Peru.

Job Market Paper

  • Internships and the School-to-Work Transition (draft coming soon)

    This paper examines the relationship between undergraduate internship participation and early labor market outcomes. Using rich retrospective survey data from recent graduates of a public university in the eastern United States across diverse majors, I estimate associations between internship completion and outcomes including starting salary, job attainment, job–major match, and unemployment. Results show that completing at least one internship is associated with about 10% higher starting salaries and a greater likelihood of obtaining a job related to one’s major. These associations are robust across specifications and similar in magnitude to the limited causal estimates reported in the literature.

Working Papers

  • Formal Entry, Informal Rents, and Labor Rationing (with Paul J. Corcuera).
    [Abstract]

    This paper studies how formal firm creation influences informality through two mechanisms: changes in market power and labor rationing. We exploit the staggered rollout of Peru’s Business Development Centers (CDE) program, which encouraged the entry of formal firms across districts and industries. A simple framework highlights that informal firms rely on both paid and unpaid family labor and may possess market power. Increased formal entry can reduce informal rents in high-barrier sectors or reallocate underemployed workers toward formal jobs in labor-abundant areas. Using administrative CDE records and ENAHO microdata, we implement an event-study design, Jackknife IV, and Shift-Share IV to estimate the impacts on profits, unpaid labor, and employment transitions.
